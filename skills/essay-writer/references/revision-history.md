# Revision history

Use this reference when the user asks what changed, why it changed, or for a revision log alongside an edit.

## Boundary

Describe observable edits and their stated purpose. Do not expose hidden chain-of-thought or claim to know an author's intention beyond the draft and request.

Keep the revised work first. The change log is a compact audit trail, not a second essay.

## Change categories

- **Meaning:** thesis, claim scope, inference, counterargument, or conclusion changed.
- **Evidence:** quotation, citation, number, source attribution, limitation, or support status changed.
- **Structure:** paragraph order, paragraph function, opening, transition, or ending changed.
- **Clarity:** reference, syntax, definition, or ambiguity improved without changing the claim.
- **Voice:** purposeful rhythm, dialect, code-switching, humour, or non-native expression intentionally retained or adjusted at the user's request.
- **Constraint:** length, citation style, audience, spelling, format, or rubric requirement addressed.

## Return format

| Location | Change | Reason | Material risk or check |
|---|---|---|---|
| Paragraph or sentence | Observable edit | Reader, argument, evidence, or constraint purpose | Verification still needed |

Include only material changes. State explicitly when a quotation, citation, number, uncertainty, or position was preserved. If an interpretive choice was necessary, label it as an assumption rather than presenting it as the writer's intention.

Never describe a revision as “humanising,” “undetectable,” or detector-optimised. A change log should help the writer review and own the result.
