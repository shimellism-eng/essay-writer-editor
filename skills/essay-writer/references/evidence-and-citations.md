# Evidence ledger and citation audit

Use this reference for a claim-source ledger, citation audit, quotation check, number check, or support-gap report.

## Purpose and boundary

The ledger makes the relationship between a draft and its evidence visible. It does not make an unsupported claim safe, and it does not authorise the model to invent source metadata.

Keep these states separate:

- **Supported:** the supplied or verified source directly supports the nearby claim at the stated scope.
- **Partially supported:** the source supports part of the claim, but the wording is broader, more certain, or more causal than the evidence.
- **Unsupported:** the available material does not support the claim.
- **Unverified:** the claim or citation may be reasonable, but the necessary source or metadata was not available for checking.
- **Inference:** the statement is the writer's interpretation of evidence, not a quotation or direct source claim.
- **Recommendation:** a proposed action or judgement that should not be presented as a source finding.

## Ledger fields

For each material claim, record only fields the material supports:

| Field | What to record |
|---|---|
| Claim ID | A short stable label such as C1 or Q2 |
| Draft claim | The exact or lightly excerpted wording |
| Claim type | Fact, quotation, number, inference, causal claim, or recommendation |
| Source | Supplied or verified source label; never guessed metadata |
| Scope | Population, place, date, unit, denominator, and condition |
| Support | Supported, partial, unsupported, unverified, inference, or recommendation |
| Citation status | Present and matched, missing, mismatched, or metadata unresolved |
| Limitation | The source's relevant design, access, sample, or measurement limit |
| Action | Keep, narrow, quote-check, source-check, cite, or remove |

Keep numbers attached to their referent, unit, numerator, denominator, and time period. Keep a direct quotation exact and distinguish a paraphrase from the source's wording.

## Citation audit

Check each citation against the claim immediately before it:

1. Does the source exist in the supplied or verified material?
2. Does it support the exact nearby claim, rather than a weaker or different one?
3. Are author, title, date, page, DOI, URL, and access date present only when known?
4. Does the requested style require metadata that is missing?
5. Is a quotation exact, short, and attached to the right source?
6. Has a source limitation been hidden by a stronger verb such as “proves,” “causes,” or “shows that all”?

Report mismatches plainly. Do not silently convert “the study observed an association” into “the study proved a cause.” If a bibliography is requested, create entries only from verified metadata and mark incomplete entries.

## Return contract

Return:

1. a concise evidence summary;
2. the ledger table;
3. citation and quotation findings;
4. numbers or causal claims needing narrowing;
5. the smallest next verification actions.

If the user asks for an edit after the audit, keep the audit separate and disclose material changes.
