# Specialised writing modes

Use the mode that matches the deliverable. Apply the shared rules for evidence, voice, uncertainty, academic integrity, and disclosure.

## Personal statement

Build around supported choices, experiences, values, and reflection. Prefer one consequential example over a résumé in paragraph form. Use only experiences the writer supplied. Do not manufacture hardship, trauma, motivation, leadership, growth, or a future plan. Keep the writer's actual voice and explain reflection through specific decisions rather than generic virtue.

Return a clear throughline: context, choice or action, what the writer learned or questioned, and why it matters to the stated application. Mark a missing personal detail instead of filling it.

### Adapting a statement for another programme

When the user supplies an existing statement and a target programme, use only two sources: the supplied statement and the programme's official pages. Keep the writer's own sentences wherever they still fit the new programme. Fix only grammar that blocks meaning, and rewrite only what must change: the programme name, courses, programme-specific plans and the few bridges to the new field. Do not reword a sentence just to make it smoother; the writer's own phrasing, including non-native phrasing, is what makes the statement recognisably theirs. When tools allow, measure how much of the result shares runs of six or more words with the original and report it. Most of the result should normally be the writer's wording; if it is not, say why. Keep every grade, award, title, organisation and duty as the statement gives it. Do not upgrade a stated goal to fit the new programme (for example, "ESG assurance" must not become "ESG and carbon assurance") and do not add outside facts about the writer, even true ones. Take course names, required components, internships and other programme details from the official pages, and name them exactly. Leave out a detail whose meaning is unclear rather than guessing it, and ask the user about it. Bridging sentences that connect the writer's experience to the new field are allowed, but keep them few and tell the user which sentences they are. Before returning, list for the user each detail removed, each item left unresolved and each bridge added.

## Scholarship essay

Answer the actual selection criteria: fit, contribution, goals, need, community, achievement, or other supplied requirements. Separate evidence of achievement from aspiration. Never invent financial hardship, family circumstances, service, awards, impact, or institutional knowledge. Make the case specific without turning the essay into promotional language.

Map each major paragraph to a criterion and identify any claim that needs the writer's confirmation or supporting record.

## Lab report

Keep method, observations, calculations, results, analysis, limitations, and conclusion distinct. Preserve units, significant figures, sample sizes, uncertainty, controls, anomalies, and the difference between measured data and interpretation. Do not fabricate readings, repeat trials, error bars, apparatus, safety steps, or a result that would make the conclusion work.

If data are absent, provide a structure or marked placeholder rather than a completed result. A polished discussion cannot compensate for missing measurements.

## Literature review

Define the question and search scope. Group sources by a meaningful debate, method, population, chronology, or concept rather than summarising one source per paragraph. Compare definitions, samples, methods, findings, limitations, and contradictions. Distinguish source claims from synthesis and identify what the available literature cannot establish.

Do not call a search comprehensive without a documented search method. Do not invent studies, citations, consensus, or gaps. Return an evidence matrix or source map before a long synthesis when the source set is large.
